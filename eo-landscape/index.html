Water: Quality, Scarcity & Infrastructure

5 companies

Freshwater is among the most material nature-related risks for businesses. EO-based water intelligence supports both operational resilience (utilities reducing waste, companies assessing water-stressed supply chains) and regulatory compliance (TNFD water dependency disclosure, CSRD environmental impact reporting).

🌿 Key Sustainability Use Cases
  • Water utilities detect and repair underground pipe leaks using SAR satellite data — preventing billions of liters of treated potable water from being lost
  • Corporations disclose water dependency and watershed risk as part of TNFD/CSRD nature reporting using satellite-derived basin stress maps
  • Companies with water-intensive operations (food, beverages, textiles, mining) assess supply chain exposure to water-stressed geographies
  • Municipal reservoir managers detect harmful algal blooms early via satellite water quality monitoring, protecting public health
  • Investors screen portfolio companies for water-related stranded asset risk in water-constrained regions

Extractives, Mining & Oil & Gas — Environmental Accountability

5 companies

Satellite monitoring holds the extractives sector accountable to environmental commitments — detecting tailings dam failures before they become disasters, verifying rehabilitation of mine sites, monitoring illegal mining, and independently tracking GHG emissions against reported figures. The use cases below emphasize environmental compliance and damage limitation, not extraction optimization.

🌿 Key Sustainability Use Cases in Extractives
  • Tailings dam monitoring: SAR satellite detects ground settlement and deformation around mining tailings ponds, providing early warning before dam failures (post-Brumadinho/Mariana era)
  • Illegal mining detection: satellite change-detection identifies unauthorized artisanal mining encroachments in protected areas, enabling enforcement
  • Mine site rehabilitation verification: satellite land cover monitoring tracks whether companies fulfill obligations to restore land to its pre-mining ecological state
  • Water contamination risk: EO detects changes in land cover and drainage patterns near mine sites, providing early warning of acid mine drainage or slurry spills into waterways
  • Independent Scope 1 emissions verification: satellite methane and CO₂ measurements give regulators and investors an independent check on operator self-reported emissions

Nature Finance Instruments & EO-Backed Verification

8 companies & initiatives
Market context: Private finance for nature reached USD 102 billion in 2024 — up elevenfold from USD 9.4 billion in 2020 (UNEP FI). Yet a USD 700 billion annual financing gap remains to reverse biodiversity loss by 2030 (Kunming-Montreal GBF Target 19). The full spectrum of instruments — biodiversity credits, blue bonds, debt-for-nature swaps, green bonds, nature performance bonds, and payments for ecosystem services — all share a critical dependency: credible, scalable monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV). Satellite-based EO is increasingly recognized as the only mechanism capable of providing continuous, independent, auditable evidence of nature outcomes at the scale these instruments require.

This section covers both dedicated EO/AI platforms serving nature finance and key instruments/programs where satellite monitoring is either already embedded or rapidly becoming foundational infrastructure. Unlike other sections, clients here include NGOs, multilateral development banks, and quasi-public institutions alongside private sector actors — reflecting the blended finance structures that characterize nature finance.

🌿 Nature Finance Instruments — EO Role by Type
  • Biodiversity Credits: Satellite habitat mapping and change detection provide the measurement baseline; ongoing EO monitoring verifies biodiversity uplift over 20–50 year credit periods, replacing costly annual field surveys and preventing greenwashing
  • Blue Bonds / Debt-for-Nature Swaps: EO-derived marine protected area monitoring verifies that conservation commitments embedded in bond covenants (e.g., protecting 30% of national waters) are being met — a requirement for interest coupon compliance and investor reporting
  • Green Bonds & Nature Performance Bonds: Satellite land cover, deforestation, and ecosystem health data provide independent KPI verification for bond reporting; performance-linked coupon step-downs require externally verifiable metrics that EO can supply
  • Payments for Ecosystem Services (PES): Satellite monitoring verifies continued provision of services (carbon sequestration, watershed protection, flood attenuation) tied to payment disbursements, removing dependence on self-reported data from landowners
  • TNFD Disclosure: All nature finance instruments increasingly require TNFD LEAP framework assessments underpinned by location-specific EO data on ecosystem condition, biodiversity intactness, and dependency mapping
